Why teams call us

Most infrastructure problems are the same four problems.

We have seen them in a bank, an insurer, an international airport and an AI research lab. The technology differs; the shape does not.

  • Nobody knows how production was built. It works, one person understands it, and that person is on holiday.
  • Deployments are a human ritual. Someone runs commands in a specific order on a Friday afternoon and hopes.
  • You find out from customers. No SLOs, no useful alerts, and dashboards that show CPU rather than whether anything is broken.
  • The cloud bill grows and nobody can say why. No allocation, no commitment strategy, no idea what is idle.

What changes after we're done

  • Every piece of infrastructure exists as reviewed code in your repository
  • Deployments happen from a pull request, with a rollback that has been tested
  • Alerts fire on user-visible symptoms, to a person who has a runbook
  • Cost is allocated per team and per environment, with commitments modelled
  • Your engineers can operate all of it without calling us

The test we hold ourselves to: your team should be able to run the platform the day we leave.
